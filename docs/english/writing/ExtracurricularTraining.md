# 课外培训热潮

假设你是高三学生李华，你关注到，近年来，从老年大学到市民夜校，各类课外培训覆盖了各年龄人群，报名火爆。校英语报正在征集关于“课外培训热潮”的投稿：将参加培训的人群进行分类（分类标准自定），具体描述每一类人群的特征及其学习动机，并基于以上分类谈谈你对这一现象所反映的社会心态的看法。（周练二作文：分类类 / 说明+议论）

## 审题要点

- 类型：分类说明 + 议论
- 三项硬性任务：
  1. 将参加培训的人群进行分类（分类标准自定，如按年龄、按目的、按身份）
  2. 描述每一类人群的特征及其学习动机
  3. 基于以上分类，谈对现象所反映的社会心态的看法
- 分类标准须自洽，能覆盖各年龄人群；不能只分类不说明标准
- 每类人群必须“特征＋动机”齐全，两者缺一不可；至少分出三类
- 看法部分必须“基于以上分类”，不能脱离分类空谈“大家都很爱学习”
- 对社会心态的分析要有独到见解，如“终身学习背后的竞争焦虑”“夜校热反映的都市孤独与社交需求”“老年大学体现的积极老龄化”等，并能自圆其说

## 一、审题分析

原题：假设你是高三学生李华，你关注到，近年来，从老年大学到市民夜校，各类课外培训覆盖了各年龄人群，报名火爆。校英语报正在征集关于“课外培训热潮”的投稿。为此，请你撰写短文，内容需包括：将参加培训的人群进行分类（分类标准自定）；具体描述每一类人群的特征及其学习动机；基于以上分类，谈谈你对这一现象所反映的社会心态的看法。

### 审题三要素

1. **现象引入**：点明“课外培训热潮”现象——从老年大学到市民夜校，报名火爆；结合题目信息亮出写作意图。
2. **硬性任务**：分类（标准自定、自洽、能覆盖各年龄人群）→ 描述每类人群“特征＋动机”（至少三类，两者缺一不可）→ 基于分类谈社会心态（不能脱离分类空谈）。
3. **议论核心论点**：
   - 社会心态分析须回扣“终身学习”“竞争焦虑”“都市孤独”“积极老龄化”等关键词；
   - 把分类细节与社会心态对接，写出认识的变化或深化；
   - 有独到见解并能自圆其说。

偏题：只罗列培训类型，不做分类或不说分类标准；只分类不写动机；看法部分脱离分类，泛泛而谈“大家都很爱学习”。

### 整体框架

- 内容 10 分 + 语言 10 分 + 结构 5 分。

- 分类标准可自定，如按年龄（青少年／中青年／老年人）、按目的（升学求职／兴趣提升／社交休闲）、按身份（学生／在职人员／退休人员）等，只要自洽即可。

## 范文一

We are witnessing a growing trend of off-hours training programs ranging from senior universities to citizen night schools, attracting learners across all age groups. Covering diverse fields, this phenomenon deserves analysis based on learners' different motives.

Elderly citizens, blessed with plenty of free time after retirement, form a large group of participants. Besides enriching their leisure time, some take courses to keep their brains active to reduce risks of cognitive decline. Others hope to avoid being isolated from modern society by mastering digital skills.

Working-age adults, occupied with work and family responsibilities, also join in. By learning practical skills such as sewing or basic appliance repair, they can cut daily expenses. Many learn without clear practical goals, reviving old hobbies to ease stress and make their limited spare time rewarding.

As for teenagers still in the education system, these out-of-school courses are mostly academically oriented, taken to improve their academic performance.

Admittedly, the craze also brings problems. Some learners follow the trend blindly and waste time and money on courses that do not suit them. Nevertheless, the fact that grown-ups keep pursuing knowledge reveals a fundamental change in people's attitude toward learning: learning is no longer restricted to classrooms. With academic achievement no longer the sole goal, diversified learning motives show people's desire for self-improvement and a meaningful life.

This renewed recognition of learning offers inspiration for our exam-oriented education system. It reminds us to create practical, rewarding and motivating learning experiences for all learners.

## 范文二

In recent years, a wide variety of extracurricular training programmes have gained great popularity across all age groups. From my perspective, participants can be roughly divided into two categories: those learning for practical gains and those for personal interest.

The first group consists of goal-oriented learners. They take courses to achieve tangible benefits. For example, high school students attend training classes to improve academic performance, while graduates and middle-aged learners sign up for courses to gain certificates or prepare for civil service exams. In contrast, the second group focuses on interest-based personal growth rather than specific practical goals. Children learning skating and elderly people taking up table tennis or calligraphy are typical examples of this group.

This simple classification reflects a social reality: learners motivated by practical benefits are far more numerous than those learning purely for hobbies. Admittedly, pursuing practical improvements is reasonable, for people need to stay competitive in a fast-changing society. However, when people treat time and money merely as tools for higher income or promotion, they may get trapped in endless utilitarian pursuit. In this case, their true feelings and well-being tend to be ignored.

Therefore, we should reflect on our attitude towards learning. Are we the masters of learning, or just slaves to practical gains? In my opinion, we should strike a balance. While career advancement and academic progress deserve our attention, they should never become the whole of life. Learning can also be a way to explore ourselves and enjoy life. Only when we balance practical needs and inner passion can we truly benefit from lifelong learning.

## 范文三

From senior universities to city night schools, training courses of all descriptions have been flourishing at an astonishing pace, enrolling learners across the entire age spectrum. To make sense of this phenomenon, participants can be categorized according to the purposes that motivate their learning.

Learners fall into three categories. The first category is utilitarian learners. What they value most are practical skills with real benefits. Some working adults take certificate courses to get promoted or stand out in job markets; some seniors learn smartphone operations to handle daily online payments. They learn to solve real-life problems and gain tangible advantages. The second group is hobby-oriented learners. They learn purely for pleasure rather than certificates. Many join painting, yoga or traditional opera classes to escape stress from busy work or study. These courses offer them emotional comfort and chances to make friends with people sharing the same interests. The third group are learners for self-exploration. They long to break personal limits and explore unknown fields. Driven by inner curiosity, they take courses just to broaden horizons, build confidence and achieve self-improvement.

This phenomenon reflects profound changes in social mentality. For one thing, more people take the initiative to invest in self-development instead of staying satisfied with the status quo. For another, people's definition of a good life has expanded. Apart from material comfort, spiritual enrichment is highly prized. Admittedly, the craze also shows people's anxiety brought by fierce competition, but it is more of positive willingness to grow. From my perspective, the popularity of all-age training marks social progress. Lifelong learning helps us keep growing in a fast-changing era.
